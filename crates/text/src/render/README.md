# Rendering

`render(&Layout, Canvas, origin, &Paint) -> Result<Image, RenderError>` rasterizes glyphs into a premultiplied RGBA8 image with a fill colour, an optional outer, centre or inner outline, an optional shadow (a shifted, box-blurred copy of the glyph coverage), an optional glow (a spread and softened halo of the same coverage), an optional underline or line-through bar per line (fill colour, 7% of the font size thick; underline top 10% below the baseline, line-through centred 30% above it) and an optional rounded background box with separate horizontal and vertical padding and a pixel offset from the content bounds. The box paints first, then glow, then shadow, then the outline/fill pair. Shadow and glow use transparent borders and a separable box blur whose radius and offsets are bounded finite pixels. The canvas admits at most 64 M pixels (256 MiB of RGBA8). Rendering uses no GPU, clock, network or filesystem input.

`render_on_arc(layout, canvas, origin, paint, curve)` is `render` with each line's glyphs placed on a circular arc: `curve` is the angle in degrees the line's arc spans, in -360..=360 (anything else refuses with `InvalidCurve`), 360 closes a full circle, positive raises the line's middle over its ends and negative lowers it. A glyph keeps its shape and turns rigidly about the centre of its advance on the baseline; the line keeps its length along the arc and stays centred on its own middle, and each line bends on its own arc. Glow, shadow and outline follow the placed glyphs; the background box and underline or line-through bars stay straight (open). `curve_displacement(degrees, width)` is the arc's sagitta, the largest a pivot moves vertically; callers size their margin from it. Zero is `render` exactly.

Optional `Paint::span_fill` changes only the RGB fill of one certified canonical byte span. Its alpha is always the base fill alpha. One whole-text coverage mask and one bounded selected-glyph mask are composited once, preserving outlines, background, decorations and antialias alpha. Uncertifiable spans refuse. With no span, the original compositing path and pixels are unchanged.
