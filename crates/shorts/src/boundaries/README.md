# Clip boundary adjustment

`clip_boundaries::snap_clip_boundaries` takes explicit shorts as vectors of `ai::clips::ClipSourceSegment` in play order, a source Transcript, its resolved LanguageProfile, media SpeechEnergyWindows and ClipBoundarySettings. Suggestion generation is optional. The caller can submit media energy decoding through the JobRunner with its cancellation token. Snapping performs no IO or project edits.

Every source edge is adjusted independently to an output-fps frame. Selection prefers a sentence gap with low RMS, then a sentence gap, then low RMS in another safe gap, then any word or segment gap. Within a word-gap tier, measured RMS ranks before distance and earlier time, even when every window exceeds the silence threshold. The threshold is an explicit amplitude ratio.

Quantization prefers a frame inside a measured safe gap within the configured tick tolerance. Otherwise it frame-rounds the lowest-RMS measured window in tolerance (or the gap itself when unmeasured), which may overlap speech by at most half a frame and is reported as `WordFade`. The fallback target can move by the tolerance clamp as well as that half-frame rounding. Clip exports apply 10 ms linear audio ramps at each piece edge, clipped to half the piece duration. Missing or incomplete native timing protects the whole segment, and proportional subtitle timing is never used for cuts. A window without a measured word or segment boundary is a typed refusal.

`SnappedShort` keeps play order, roles, source-order ranks and repair metadata, with adjusted ranges, summed duration and signed original and adjusted tick deltas for both edges of every segment. Inputs are unchanged. Invalid candidates, source-order ranks, multiple or misplaced hooks, overlapping source ranges, post-adjustment overlap and duration violations are typed refusals, and a call is atomic. Positive total and piece duration limits and a segment limit in 1..=32 are caller-selected. At most 32 shorts and 360,001 energy windows are accepted. `BoundaryFailure` identifies candidate, safe-frame, overlap or duration refusal with zero-based short and segment indices (whole-short failures use segment zero).
