# Audio clock and headroom

`audio/` owns mix, cleanup, retime, loudness, limiter and oversampling.

`mix_audio(project, absolute_start_sample, frames, channels, sources, hooks)` is the preview and export owner. Project rate defaults to 48 kHz and admits 8 to 192 kHz. Output is mono or stereo, default stereo. Every source and item tick boundary rounds once to the absolute output sample grid using Nearest. Silence fills gaps and half-open inactive intervals. A block admits at most 65,536 samples per channel and 4,096 audible spans. Stereo stays L/R, mono duplicates at unity, stereo to mono averages L/R. Other source layouts are unsupported.

Gain is f32 with per-item dB gain and mute evaluated through project's item-local animation evaluator at each absolute output sample, so block size and other tracks cannot change it. Video source audio uses the same volume and mute controls. `source_audio_enabled=false` excludes it regardless of picture visibility. Nonfinite input or overflow refuses the mix. Clip fades (`project::Fades`) multiply each item's gain per absolute sample, after volume keyframes and before summing and the master chain. Preview and export are bit-identical for any block partition.

Media resamples each continuous source from its original clock, keeping filter phase across block partitions, splits and trims. Seeking backwards restarts that source cursor. Source-rate decode and AAC priming are media-owned; render never reapplies priming.

## Retime

A retimed item plays source position `n * speed` at output sample `n` of its original processing window. A split keeps that window and advances the output offset, so resampling phase and WSOLA grains do not restart at the cut. Sample rounding and the implicit edge envelope use the retained window; explicit fades keep their project-owned split offsets.

A reversed item reads its window's samples in reverse order before any rate conversion, so reversal never changes pitch; it always goes through the retimer, even at 1x. With `maintain_pitch` off, a streaming resampler reads the position with linear interpolation, area-averaging ceil(speed) taps above 1x; pitch follows speed. With it on, WSOLA keeps pitch: 40 ms squared-Hann grains at a 20 ms output hop, source offsets searched within +/-8 ms, summed with window-weight normalization so item edges keep unity gain and source outside the trimmed window never leaks in.

Output depends only on the item, speed and source, so block partitions and seeks give identical audio, and source reads only move forward. The mixer keeps one bounded, leased retimer per active retimed item; the lease covers maximum-block source read-ahead sized by the item's fastest stretch (up to 100x), pull buffers, overlap-add state and output staging, and a budget that cannot hold it refuses before synthesis. WSOLA checks cancellation between grains, including after a source pull. Cancellation discards the mixer cursor so retry replays deterministically.

Stream copy is refused for retimed items. Video frames use the nearest earlier source frame at the mapped source time. Perceptual quality on speech or music is unmeasured.

## Source cleanup

An audio or video item's `cleanup` chain (`project::AudioCleanup`, at most 8 effects) runs on its source samples after retime and before item volume, fades and automation, so preview and export share it. Source media is never changed. Effects run in list order; each keeps `wet_percent` of its processed signal and the rest of its input. A disabled effect or a zero mix is skipped, and an all-bypassed chain is bit-identical to no chain.

Kinds: hum notches at the mains frequency's harmonics (1 to 10, Q 1 to 100, harmonics at or above 0.45 times the sample rate omitted); Butterworth high-pass and low-pass (20 to 20 000 Hz); low shelf at 200 Hz and high shelf at 4 kHz (-24 to +24 dB); ten octave-spaced peaking bands at 31.5 Hz to 16 kHz (-24 to +24 dB); and up to 8 parametric bands (peaking, low or high shelf, notch; 20 to 20 000 Hz, -30 to +30 dB, Q 0.1 to 30). Coefficients are RBJ cookbook designs in f64 transposed direct form II, flushed below 1e-30.

Every section is second order, zero latency and sample-recursive, so the state after a block depends only on the samples before it. A block that continues the previous block of the same item keeps the state; any other start (a seek, or a gap while the item is muted or outside the timeline) restarts it. The mixer clears the chain state with its other per-item state on replay, so preview, export and any block partition produce identical samples. The limiter's delay is unchanged.

Two more kinds delay their signal. `noise_reduction` (`reduction_db` 0 to 40, `threshold_db` 0 to 20, `smoothing_percent` 0 to 100) is short-time spectral gating: frames of 2048 samples at up to 50 kHz (4096 up to 100 kHz, 8192 above), 75 % overlap, square-root Hann windows that sum to a constant, so an open gate is an exact delayed identity. The floor of every frequency bin is tracked continuously as the minimum of the smoothed per-bin power over a 1.5 s window, bias compensated by 2.5, so there is no learning step; a bin that does not rise `threshold_db` over the floor is attenuated by `reduction_db`, with per-bin attack and release (the release follows `smoothing_percent`) and a three-tap smoothing across frequency against musical noise. Frames that still hold the zeroed start of the buffer are not counted, and the gate stays open until one 0.19 s sub-window of real frames has been seen, so an item that starts on speech is not gated on its first words. Sources: Griffin and Lim 1984 (STFT analysis and overlap-add resynthesis), Boll 1979 (spectral subtraction), Martin 2001 (minimum statistics), constants adapted from the structure reference's `audio-dsp` DeNoise. `declick` (`threshold` 1 to 100, `complexity` 1 to 100) is click removal in the sample domain: per 512-sample block an autoregressive model (order 8 to 32 by `complexity`) is fitted by Levinson-Durbin over a Hann-windowed 1152-sample span, samples whose prediction error exceeds `4 + 0.2 * threshold` robust deviations mark a click, marks at most four samples apart merge, and each region up to 16 to 64 samples (by `complexity`) is rebuilt from forward and backward prediction cross-faded; longer regions are transients and stay. Sources: Vaseghi and Rayner 1990, Godsill and Rayner 1998, block layout adapted from the same structure reference. The filters and the FFT (radix-2, planned once per size) are in `cleanup/`.

Latency design. A stage that delays its signal reports its delay (one frame for noise reduction and for de-reverb, 640 samples for de-click, none for de-esser, never a function of the block size) and the chain's delay is the sum over its enabled stages. The mix graph compensates it per item by reading the item's source that many samples ahead: after a restart it feeds the chain the first `latency` samples to pay the delay, then each block `[a, b)` takes source `[a + latency, b + latency)`, with silence past the item's end, and the chain returns exactly `[a, b)`. The item therefore stays sample-aligned with its picture and every other item in preview and export with no change to `latency_samples`, which stays the limiter's. `AudioMixer::cleanup_latency_samples` reports the longest chain for callers that want it (the compensation is already applied). A mix below 100 % holds the dry signal back by the stage's delay, so dry and wet add at the same instant. Offline export runs the same code on the same samples, so preview equals export, and the chain's output depends only on the samples fed, so any block partition and any replayed seek give identical samples.

Measured limits (synthetic, `crates/render/tests/it/audio_mixer.rs`): with `reduction_db` 20 on white noise at 0.02 with 1 kHz bursts at 0.3, the noise-only half seconds fall by more than 10 dB, the bursts stay within 1 dB and nothing exceeds the source peak; with the gate open the output equals the plain mix within 1e-4 per sample; a 1 kHz tone through either delayed effect matches the plain mix within 1e-3, which a one-sample shift would break (0.026). De-click cuts the error of a tone with 30 two-sample clicks by more than 20 dB. Not measured: perceptual quality on real speech or music, the cost per block (the combined build measures it), and stationary tonal content, which minimum statistics treats as noise and attenuates.

`dereverb` (`amount_percent` 0 to 100, `rt60_seconds` 0.1 to 5) is late-reverberation suppression on the noise reduction's frame layout: the late power of each bin is predicted from its smoothed power 50 ms earlier as `exp(-2 * delta * Td)` with `delta = 3 ln(10) / RT60`, and removed by over-subtracted spectral gain `max(1 - (1 + amount) * late / power, floor)`, the floor reaching -18 dB at 100 %, with fast opening, slower closing and three-tap smoothing across frequency. At 0 % every gain is one and the stage is an exact delayed identity; its delay is one frame, accounted like noise reduction's. A steady signal is partly taken for reverberation (gain `1 - (1 + amount) * exp(-2 * delta * Td)`), so the RT60 should match the room. Sources: Lebart, Boucher and Denbigh 2001 and Habets 2007 (spectral subtraction with the Polack decay model), constants adapted from the structure reference's `audio-dsp` DeReverb. `deess` (`frequency_hz` 2000 to 12000, `threshold_db` -40 to 0, `reduction_db` 0 to 24) is a zero-latency dynamic high shelf: a Butterworth high-pass at the frequency detects the band, its smoothed power against the broadband power of all channels (so the cut is stereo-linked) gives an excess over the threshold, twice that excess capped at the reduction is the target cut, smoothed with a 1 ms attack and a 60 ms release, and an RBJ high shelf at the frequency applies it; at zero cut the shelf is an exact identity. White noise or hiss counts as sibilance to it, as to any relative-level de-esser. Detector constants adapted from the structure reference's DeEsser.

Voice enhancement (`project::voice_enhancement_effects`) is a preset over this chain, not a stage of its own: 80 Hz high-pass, de-reverb (40 %, RT60 0.3 s), noise reduction, de-esser (6 kHz, -12 dB, 8 dB) and a two-band equaliser (2.5 kHz +4 dB, 10 kHz shelf +2 dB); each effect carries the preset marker and an ordinary id. It adds the de-reverb and noise reduction frames of latency (4096 samples at 48 kHz), which the graph compensates, and no gain stage, so the loudness planner below meters its output and never normalizes twice. Metric and pass line (`voice_enhancement_preset_lifts_speech_over_noise_without_pumping_and_bypass_is_identity`, synthetic voiced bursts over noise and rumble): speech-to-noise ratio up by at least 6 dB, speech level within 3 dB, 50 ms gain spread inside speech under 3 dB, peak under 0.9, bypass bit-identical; measured +17.1 dB, -1.7 dB, 0.24 dB and 0.27. Stage limits: `deesser_cuts_a_sibilant_band_leaves_the_voiced_stack_and_bypass_is_identity` (a steady 5.5, 7 and 8.5 kHz band falls by at least 4 dB at 8 dB reduction, the 140 Hz harmonic stack moves by less than 1 dB; measured -4.8 dB and 0 dB) and `dereverb_lowers_a_decaying_tail_keeps_the_direct_onset_and_bypass_is_identity` (a tail decaying at RT60 0.8 s loses at least 4 dB between 0.5 s and 1.0 s at 100 %, the first 10 ms stay within 1 dB; measured -13.6 dB and 0 dB).

Not yet implemented: an Audio tab control for equaliser bands (the reference capture shows none, so the equaliser kinds are reachable through the `audio.*_cleanup_effect` commands only), and keyframed cleanup parameters (parameters are static per project snapshot).

## Master chain

`AudioMixer::new(snapshot, channels, fade_ms, hooks)` keeps bounded DSP state for sequential realtime or offline blocks. The linked master chain is a -1 dBFS threshold, 20:1 hard knee, one-pole 1 ms attack / 120 ms release, 5 ms lookahead plus eight samples of FIR detector alignment, and a final hard sample clamp. A Kaiser-windowed polyphase detector estimates intersample peaks; this is not a certified true-peak ceiling. Post-limiter gain is 0.98, applied to below-threshold signals too. Every path applies the chain, including quiet offline exports.

`latency_samples` reports the full delay. It is removed by priming and feeding ahead, so returned PCM stays on the requested sample grid with no leading delay or lost final samples. Gaps and empty content stay bit-exact zero.

Retain one mixer per immutable snapshot. A discontinuous seek replays from timeline zero in bounded, cancellable blocks to rebuild the release envelope, so cost grows with seek position. `mix_audio` is a convenience for independent windows over the same graph. Playback owns one mixer per request epoch, export one per operation. Sources and DSP leases are caller-owned. A failed read invalidates the graph cursor before retry.

## Loudness

Normalization (EBU R128 / BS.1770-4) is a two-pass planner over the same graph. `measure_loudness(project, sources, hooks)` reads the project's clip and master targets.

Pass one mixes each audible clip alone with its volume, mute and automation, without master fader, mute, limiter or other gains, and meters it. Clip gain is `min(target - integrated, ceiling - true peak)`. Pass two meters the whole pre-limiter mix with those clip gains applied, and derives the master gain the same way against `min(true_peak_db, -1 dBFS)`.

The meter is K-weighted at the project rate, 400 ms blocks on a 100 ms hop, absolute gate -70 LUFS, relative gate -10 LU, true peak from the limiter's 4x/2x polyphase detector. Measurement is stereo (mono duplicates at unity), so a mono export of stereo-measured material reads up to 3 dB lower. Silence, clips under 400 ms and gains within 0.05 dB are exact unity. Gains clamp to +/-60 dB. An unreachable target lands below target rather than clipping.

The planner reports progress through `Stage::Mix`, honours cancellation and returns `LoudnessReport` (gains plus measured numbers; the engine owns its key). `project.settings.loudness.measured` holds the gains. `AudioMixer` and `mix_audio` apply the clip gain beside item volume and the master gain to the summed mix ahead of the limiter, whose 0.98 post gain stays in the chain, so final loudness is 0.18 dB under target. Gains apply sample-accurately and do not change timing.

## Export audio

`ExportSettings::audio_cut_fade_ms` optionally applies linear ramps at audible item edges on the absolute sample grid (0 disables, maximum 1000 ms, capped at half item duration). Clip batch exports use 10 ms. All included export audio runs through the graph and encodes AAC at 192 kbit/s for MP4/MOV or Opus for WebM, even when video packets copy. Media owns codec priming and FIFO.

A speed curve item retimes through the same `Retimer` with a `RateMap::Curve` of sample-grid knots instead of a constant ratio. Output sample `n` reads the map's source position (linear inside each stretch, so the position is continuous at every key), averaging `ceil(local speed)` taps while pitch follows speed. With `maintain_pitch` on, WSOLA grain centres come from the same map, so pitch holds through every stretch and no grain restarts at a key. Knots that collapse onto the previous one at the project rate are dropped. Block partitions and seeks give identical audio as for a constant rate.
